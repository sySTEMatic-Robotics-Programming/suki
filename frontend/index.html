how this reads

The four legs as they sit on the robot, seen from above, front at the top. Each bar is the servo's whole electrical travel, 500–2500 µs; the bright part is what this joint is allowed, and the marker is where it is now. Red is a joint pressed against its own end stop, which stalls it. The channel map, the min/max fields and the hardware test live in the list.

what these columns do

channel is which of the driver's sixteen outputs a motor is plugged into — picking one another motor already has swaps the two, carrying calibration, position and saved poses with them. Either way the robot goes limp first, because those outputs are about to carry a different servo's pulse.

inverted mirrors the number onto the pulse; the wire is unchanged across the toggle, so nothing jerks. on off holds the channel actively limp. on wire is what the servo really sees — flipping inverted and dragging again is how you find which way a motor turns.

Leg limits & manual move the knee's allowed range against the thigh, and the two sliders that move inside it

when the gap is actually waited out

Only when one leg stops and another starts — that is when the rail needs to recover. A leg moving on its own has nobody to hand over to, so it runs straight through at the speed limit. The motion loop is 50 Hz, so anything under 20 ms is the same as 0.
